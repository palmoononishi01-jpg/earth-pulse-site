How to read this ranking

  • The year is 2024, the latest year in which at least 90% of the maximum number of countries have a value (the latest year with any value is 2025).
  • "200 countries" is the number of countries with a value in this year. Ranks are positions among those countries, not among every country in the world.
  • Countries with equal values share a rank. "Rank 10 years earlier" is the rank for the same indicator in 2014; it is "—" if the country had no value that year.
  • A larger or smaller value does not mean good or bad. Differences in the size of countries, such as population and area, also matter.

What the indicator means and how to compare

Gross domestic product converted to US dollars at annual official exchange rates (current prices).

The total value added produced within a country over a period (gross domestic product, GDP), converted to US dollars at each year's exchange rate, in nominal terms. It is a standard measure of the size of a country's economic activity.

GDP per capita divides GDP by population, and GDP growth is the real rate of increase with price changes removed. The total shows the size of the economy and the per-capita value is an average per person, so they mean different things.

Because it is a nominal value in US dollars, it is affected by exchange rates and price changes. It is not a comparison of living standards or purchasing power between countries.
