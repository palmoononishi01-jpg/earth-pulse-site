About natural events

How to read natural event information

The EARTH PULSE globe overlays, in near real time, earthquake information from the USGS (U.S. Geological Survey) and information on volcanoes, wildfires, storms, floods and more from NASA EONET. This page explains how to read it and where its limits are.

Not an official warning. This is not an official warning. For evacuation and safety decisions, follow the official information of your country, local authority, and national weather and disaster agencies.

How to read each kind of event

Earthquakes

Earthquakes are shown from the hypocenter information published by the USGS. The size and color of a circle show the magnitude, the size of the earthquake itself.

Information right after an earthquake is automatic and not yet reviewed, so location and size can be revised later. Depending on the period, small earthquakes are not part of the feed.

Magnitude and intensity are different

Magnitude is a single number for the size (energy) of the earthquake itself and does not depend on where it is measured. Intensity describes the strength of shaking at a particular place, and varies with distance from the epicenter and ground conditions.

This site shows magnitude, not intensity. Intensity scales also differ between countries (for example the Japan Meteorological Agency scale).

Volcanoes, wildfires, storms, floods and more

These come from NASA EONET, and events currently listed as "open" are shown. Wildfires are those observed in the last 30 days.

A position is a representative point, not the extent of damage or impact. An event missing from EONET may still be happening.

Sources and limits

  • It is near real time. It can lag the source by minutes to hours and is not exhaustive.
  • "Stale data" means updates have stopped. It does not mean the event has ended.
  • A source that could not be fetched is shown as "Not loaded". That does not mean zero events.
  • There are no forecasts or warnings. The path shown for a storm is a record of past observations, not a forecast.

Data sources

  • USGS Earthquake Hazards Program: Public domain (U.S. Government work). Source: U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)
  • NASA Earth Observatory Natural Event Tracker (EONET) v3: NASA Open Data (no commercial restriction, citation requested). Source: NASA EONET.
