FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Where does the data come from?

Statistics come from the open data of the World Bank (WDI), the Global Carbon Project (CO2 emissions) and Ember (electricity). Earthquakes come from the USGS, and volcanoes, wildfires, storms and similar events from NASA EONET. The full list is on the Data sources page.

How often are the statistics updated?

The statistics are annual data, imported regularly as providers publish them. The foot of each page shows the dataset version and the date it was generated. Natural events are near real-time information updated every few minutes to a few tens of minutes.

Why does a country show "No data"?

Either the provider has no value for that country and year, or it publishes it under a different definition. This site never fills gaps with 0 or estimates. The latest year can also differ by country.

Is the total in "rank X of Y" the number of countries in the world?

No. It is the number of countries with a value for that indicator in that year. Countries without a value are not ranked, so the number changes with the year and the indicator.

What does "Calculated" mean?

A value this site computed from the providers' figures. Population density, for example, is total population divided by land area, calculated only when both values exist for the same year.

Can I use the natural event display for evacuation decisions?

No. It is not an official warning. Follow the official information of your country, local authority and weather or disaster agency for any safety decision.

What is the difference between magnitude and intensity?

Magnitude is the size of the earthquake itself; intensity is the strength of shaking at a place. See the page about natural events for details.

May I quote the numbers or images?

The statistics and natural event data each carry the provider's license. When quoting, include the attribution shown on the Data sources page.
